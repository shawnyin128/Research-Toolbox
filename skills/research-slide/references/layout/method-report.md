# Method reports: preserve the reasoning object

Choose between three observed method/report families by what the audience must follow. A method explanation does not require a newly invented flowchart. The ordered outline itself can be the scientific object. Inspect the selected image and its science-free starter before placing content.

Decimal/lettered outlines are legitimate author grammar. Preserve the actual order and nested ownership from current facts. Distinguish a18pt stage heading with14pt detail from a flat14pt conceptual outline; they are different families. The number of stages and details belongs to current science. Do not transfer source method identifiers or counts.

A paper equation retains its own typography, notation, identifiers and conditions. A complete excerpt can remain independently styled even when the surrounding outline is Arial. Native deck-text measurements do not recover raster equation fonts. Redraw equations faithfully when editability is needed, rather than converting the method into a normalized operator diagram.

For algorithm reports, empirical plots corroborate the premise and a local illustration explains the operation. Preserve those distinct roles and their scientific identity. Bold operation leads belong to narrative hierarchy; they do not establish a requirement for boxes, arrows or a full-width dependency graph. Conversely, use an [operator graph](operator-graph.md) when dependencies themselves must be traced. Source17's lower objects approach the footer and canvas edge; contain current objects with minimal local changes rather than reproducing overlap.

Exact native fonts, baseline origins, clip frames and raster placements are in [broad measurements](../author-examples/measurements-broad.json); these contain source copy and are consulted only for a measurement question. Runtime layout uses the science-free [focused starters](../author-examples/focused-layout-starters.json). Keep the current scientific scope and truth even where an original sentence or illustration was imperfect.
