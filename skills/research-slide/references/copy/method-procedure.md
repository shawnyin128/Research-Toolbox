# Copy — method outlines and procedures

Choose the grammar by what the audience needs to read. These forms are not interchangeable:

- **Narrative algorithm:** premise or high-level idea, followed by the actual operations. Short role headings and inline action leads can organize connected bullets. A supporting inset supplies a local example or evaluation, without becoming the main explanation.
- **Paper-method outline:** retain the paper/method identity, list its conceptual components, and nest the necessary explanatory clauses under the relevant component. Numbering can organize concepts without asserting that every item is an execution dependency. An independent equation excerpt supports the outline; it need not be expanded into a pipeline or symbol-by-symbol lesson.
- **Numbered procedure:** use ordered stage names or actions with subordinate concrete details. Preserve real inputs, outputs and necessary settings in those details. Sequence and nesting can carry the procedure without an extra diagram. A source's mix of noun labels and action phrases need not be regularized into full parallel sentences.
- **Operator graph:** use when distinct operators, branches or update paths must be traced. Topology carries those dependencies; prose describes the framework and key distinctions rather than giving a verbal tour of each edge. See [mechanism text](mechanism-text.md).

Keep method names, technical distinctions, constants and scope from the current facts wherever the approved page makes them recoverable. A compact phrase may combine operation and setting. Equations can retain types and products; captions or local labels can define a new mapping once. Do not import the source's example settings, imply achieved benefits from a motivation, or add a result/takeaway to an outline that only reports a method. For a scientific table or plot whose evidence is the primary account, use [experimental claims](experimental-claims.md) instead.
