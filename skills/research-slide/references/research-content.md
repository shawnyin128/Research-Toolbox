# Scope in author mode

When an author reference is supplied, its measured text sizes and working-report structure take precedence over generic presentation defaults below. A private research page need not contain a claim title, recommendation or decision.

# Research content

## Choose the narrative

| Talk | Default progression |
|---|---|
| Journal club | Research question, needed background, approach, key evidence, limitations, assessment |
| Progress update | Goal, previous state, new work, results, unresolved issues, next experiment |
| Conference talk | Problem, gap, central idea, mechanism, decisive evidence, scoped conclusion |
| Defense | Research question, contributions, methods and evidence, limitations, synthesis |
| Proposal | Problem, hypothesis, approach, evaluation plan, risks, milestones |

Adapt this progression to the material. Do not create nonexistent results or next steps. Budget time by actual complexity rather than a rigid slides-per-minute rule. Reserve detailed derivations, extra results, and anticipated questions for backups when useful.

## Ground every claim

- Associate each number and borrowed figure with a verified source location: page, section, table, figure, dataset cell, or experiment artifact.
- Distinguish reported results, independently calculated quantities, interpretations, and proposals. Keep uncertainty, assumptions, and scope attached to the claim.
- Preserve model, dataset, sample size, denominator, metric, units, time window, baseline, and error-bar definitions where relevant. Do not imply causal or statistically significant effects without evidence.
- When sources conflict, record the discrepancy and qualify or omit the disputed claim. Do not silently select the more favorable number.
- Put readable short citations near borrowed evidence and full references in notes or a reference appendix. Use only verified bibliographic details.
- Keep the conclusions or take-home slide as the final visible slide when appropriate. Place detailed references and backups afterward. Hide backups only when the destination supports it and read back that state.

## Draw the mechanism

Show inputs, outputs, data flow, transformations, or a concrete example. Use diagrams to explain operation, rather than displaying an unexplained row of component boxes. Define notation before using it. Keep equations consistent with the source and verify dimensions and operation order.

Prefer native editable PowerPoint equations when a supported OMML workflow exists. Verify the actual rendered equation as well as its source. Do not promise native math for a backend that only supports rendered equation images. Preserve a LaTeX representation alongside any rasterized or vector equation fallback and disclose it.

## Make evidence readable

Use native charts for verified data when fidelity and backend support permit. Keep existing scientific figures when redrawing would lose meaning or misrepresent uncertainty. Preserve image aspect ratio and all meaningful labels. Never use generative imagery to represent exact scientific plots or quantitative evidence.

Highlight the relevant comparison in a plot or caption. Keep method colors, marker styles, metric direction, and numerical precision consistent. Default to readable room-presentation body sizes around 20–24 pt, then adapt to canvas and use case. Allow denser group-meeting figures when still readable at the intended viewing scale. Do not shrink prose to force an unsuitable layout.

## 将工作记录交给制作者

交接材料分清三类：作者明说的操作、图中直接可读的现象、整理人推断的实现。比如图上画了未来位置被遮住，只能据此标示因果边界；除数如何处理被遮住的项，需要作者的公式或代码。没有具体数值的示意图不能反推出实测排名。保留原有任务缩写、方向箭头、文献归属以及假设提出者的人称。若作者写的是“我可能贴错了这组数”，原页承担的是追溯记录的功能；将其改写成客观故障结论会丢失来源。
