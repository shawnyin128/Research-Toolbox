# Author layout grammar: compose the research record, not a default slide

Use this after the profile and two functional examples. The actual example's hierarchy takes precedence over these approximate recipes; a native helper must not introduce a layer merely for convenience. These recipes were refined on development cases, so reproducing those cases is regression evidence; a new experiment is needed to test transfer. Coordinates are approximate pt regions for 720×405. They are starting relationships, not a rigid template or permission to change science.

Read [working prose and exact levels](author-prose.md). When a reference uses native bullets and nested qualifiers, preserve that connected paragraph structure; replacing it with separated polished paragraphs changes the author grammar.

## Before placing text: separate content from guardrails

Research notes contain (a) what the researcher wants to discuss, (b) facts and evidence needed to interpret it, (c) instructions constraining the maker. Only a/b belong visibly on the slide. “Do not invent a next step”, “do not infer a slowdown from shares”, “no arrays are available”, and “do not claim significance” constrain behavior; they are not automatically prose to add as footnotes. Add a visible caveat when a visible claim would otherwise mislead this collaborator audience. Do not repeat the title, research question and explanation of the same term in three different text boxes. Keep the raw source's uncertainty; attribute a researcher's interpretation when needed, without replacing their working voice with a methodology disclaimer.

## Pick the whole composition

### Definition of a stored representation

Use a scientific object with short explanatory bullets. When the first object or labels already name the format, a separate large title may be redundant; example 08 demonstrates a real untitled working page. A definition does not need a tutorial banner, section headers for every precision level, a “1:1” callout plus a repeated explanatory line plus a second expansion unless these enable a needed operation. Prefer a large native representation diagram on one side (roughly x25–295) and ordinary explanatory bullets on the other (x330–700). A smaller scale object or matching grid can sit below its representation. Maintain one-to-one mapping through shared geometry/color or indices; do not over-explain it with multiple visual layers. A block denotes a group; draw every inner element when the audience needs to count/trace them, otherwise label its size clearly. Never invent bad grammar or unlabelled objects to imitate a source flaw.

### Result table plus a worked transformation

A configuration comparison and a sequence of transformations are different reading tasks. Use **one results table and an actual operation sequence**, rather than two similarly styled tables with the sequence buried as row names. Reserve about one third of the width for vertically aligned native vectors/matrices, stage labels next to them, arrows between stages and the typeset factor/formula at the relevant operation. The remaining two thirds can hold compact Experiment/Results prose above the results table. A useful initial allocation is left x5–490, right x510–700, with the results table in the left lower region. Adapt the prose/table heights to the number of rows. Keep all required numerical columns; do not enlarge the results table to the entire canvas if it crowds the transformation into a tiny second table.

Worked-vector digits are an explanatory mechanism, not a second dense results table: the positional references use body-sized numerical labels around14–16pt. Start near that level with a stronger ~1pt scientific outline, while a dense results table may use10pt and .5pt lines. Do not shrink both evidence forms to the same tiny default.

Keep channel positions aligned vertically. The same channel must remain in the same column through input, scaled vector, clustered vector, ratio and output. A reader should follow one channel without translating row labels back into a mental operation. Refer to [author components](author-components.md) for a native grouped table and vector pipeline. Use real math scripts/fractions or carefully aligned editable mathematical runs; raw underscores/carets are working input notation, not finished math.

### Runtime ablation / overhead observation

Use a direct topic title, then a compact research question and reported observation together in the upper region. A short “TLDR” with nested comparison statements is legitimate working language when it fits the record. Keep a question/setup and its two scope comparisons in one ordinary bullet flow rather than creating a new H2 heading plus widely separated paragraph blocks. Keep the full evidence table across the lower region (roughly x12–675,y230–335), not automatically at the center followed by a second explanatory essay. Preserve whitespace between observation and data if the small table does not need it. The central contrast here is usually the measured operation versus the full run/step; define the comparison precisely in a short line. Add no warning that merely repeats a maker constraint after the result is already bounded.

Choose the local table variant from the period/family. A fine blue-gray border and dark-blue header/emphasis can identify the configuration being investigated; a plain black grid is also present in this archive. Emphasis may mean **configuration under discussion**, not “wins every metric.” Identify that intent privately. Do not suppress a useful focus-row mark solely because it is not best on every metric; never imply a winner without evidence.

### Table-led follow-up with little prose

A topic title and one compact method change with its formula may be sufficient. Model/precision can be a native merged stub in the table. Group related columns into Perplexity and Tasks with a two-row header when that grouping exists in the data. A narrow pale row mark and a few bold relevant cells can locate the comparison. Do not add separate setup bullets, a generic subtitle or an unrequested conclusion to fill space. The table's evidence is the end of the page.

## Research tables are structured evidence

For the quantization result family, grouped gray headers and merged condition cells are part of how the author explains a result, not optional decoration. Preserve hierarchy: Model / configuration (strategy, alpha, beta); metric families; metric columns. Use one merged model/precision stub for shared conditions, and merge repeated method labels when it makes the controlled sweep explicit. Do not flatten this into a default white one-row table solely because a convenience renderer supports only that form. Use direct native table operations or the grouped-table helper instead.

Typical table cells on a dense page are 8–10pt with tight padding; the body may remain 14pt. Selective red/blue text can contrast a baseline and a reported improvement in the exact affected cells. Do not color every value or flatten the author's gray hierarchy into an unrelated UI theme. Retain the short original metric labels; use a compact note for an acronym only if the collaborator needs it.

## Local branding and final visual audit

The supplied archive has multiple local families. Quantization/clustering experimental examples 01–03 use the SAI Lab mark and grouped gray comparison tables; runtime examples 06–07 use NYU. Notation/format pages can be sparse and use NYU. Prefer a same-experiment/period reference, and preserve the current deck's actual footer when continuing one. Do not apply one convenient default footer and one table style to every case.

Before finalizing, compare the thumbnail silhouettes with the selected reference: where prose ends, where the table starts, whether an operation is visible as a sequence, whether the evidence is the same size relative to the page. Then inspect exact cells/line breaks at full size. Each new component must help perform a scientific action; removing decorative layers must not remove data. Match the author's composition while keeping the evidence at least as recoverable as the reference.

## Evidence scale and comparison emphasis before freeze

For notes beside a supplied scientific plot, reserve roughly half the canvas width initially (about350–380pt on720pt), extending to the right edge with a deliberate small margin. Keep the complete source aspect ratio. Read its smallest condition/axis/legend label at final scale; if those labels are smaller than the functional example, allocate the plot more width before spreading short notes over the left half. A large empty left-bottom area does not justify shrinking scientific evidence. Plain purpose / Observation / nested observations can fit a narrower note column. Keep the actual condition text inside the intact plot rather than repeating it in more body text.

Before rendering a native results table, write a private emphasis map: which cells demonstrate the reported comparison, which operating point is selected, and which exception bounds the headline. Use consistent cell emphasis across the relevant comparisons, including an exception row. A selected operating point can have one restrained light row fill while individual best values remain bold; this does not mean the selected row wins every metric. Do not suppress the operating-point cue because other rows win individual metrics. A single selection fill is different from a heatmap. Runtime/overhead focus tables can use dark-blue bold header text, thin blue-gray rules and a pale focus row when the local family calls for it; a native helper's default black table is not a styling decision. Preserve readable margins and no invented units.

For the SAI experimental family, explicitly preserve local red/blue comparison cues and pale definition captions where they serve the controlled comparison. Default black text everywhere loses a recurring scientific reading cue. Protect complete method tokens and keep the caption separate from the logo; do not reproduce narrow-cell token damage.
