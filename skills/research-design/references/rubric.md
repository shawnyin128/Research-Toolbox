# Fork Test Quality Checks

Check observable content before presenting a design. Use each dimension to identify a concrete repair or evidence gap; a self-assigned total is not evidence that the design works.

| Dimension | Evidence to inspect |
|---|---|
| Competing explanations | M1 preserves the user's idea; M2 is charitable; M0 names a concrete null/artifact. Assumptions and possible mixtures are explicit. |
| Prediction separation | The matrix contains an intervention and metric whose predictions differ for a stated reason; unknown or shared predictions remain labeled. |
| Minimal substrate | The design preserves the disputed mechanism at the smallest sufficient level and states both supported conclusions and limits. |
| Decision discipline | Result patterns map to conditional belief updates and actions; kill/expansion thresholds are consistent, branches are mutually exclusive or have stated precedence, and rules are observable, scoped, and declared before the deciding run. |
| Evidence integrity | Source facts, inferences, and new hypotheses are distinguishable; source pointers and missing evidence are visible where material. |
| Test validity | Intervention/evaluator checks, relevant controls, independent units, and uncertainty can distinguish an informative result from invalid or inconclusive evidence. |

Some gaps cannot be compensated for by strengths elsewhere. Unsupported source claims, an invalid intervention/evaluator, a substrate that removes the mechanism, or no feasible predictive separation prevent a confident mechanism claim. Repair what the evidence allows; otherwise present a provisional or non-diagnostic design and name the missing condition. Do not fabricate alternatives, thresholds, results, or certainty to “pass” the checks.
